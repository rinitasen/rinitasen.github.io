Does Your Content Speak Your Buyer's Language?

B2B buyers are tough to please. You’d probably agree with me when I say that they are so neck deep in their own world that they can smell (and reject) content that does not speak to them.

What you need to stand is not just a writer, but a writer who is a strong strategist and a curator.

Here are a few case studies that would walk you through why I am that 3-in-1 consultant that fits the bill.

The Strategist

In case you’re wondering, I didn’t just wake up one day and become a B2B content strategist, and that too for tech domains.

First, there was the Master’s degree in Computer Engineering, which, by the way, is my way of telling you that if you’ve got a product architecture that’s too heavy for the average content leader to comprehend, I’m the person you’re looking for.

And then, there was a decade of working with industrial tech leaders, enabling them to improve their sales, strategize their GTM initiatives, and enhance their customer satisfaction.

Read through to know about a couple of these projects that woke the strategist in me that could shape your next content marketing project.

01 — Mapping the Road Ahead for an Automotive Manufacturer

A South Asian automotive manufacturer needed to understand the market landscape before committing to their go-to-market strategy.

The Challenge

  • How safe were the roads?
  • What was the government doing about vehicle and road safety technology?
  • Where were competitors placing their bets on vehicle automation?

The Solution

I conducted a comprehensive primary and secondary research study, interviewing government officials, industry experts, competitors, and internal stakeholders to build a complete picture of the landscape.

The analysis was structured using SWOT and AHP frameworks to prioritize findings and surface actionable strategic direction.

The Outcome

The final delivery was a detailed research report and a crisp strategy slide deck that fed directly into the client's go-to-market planning.

The Takeaway

A thorough market study is vital to any successful content marketing project. Sure, you may already have your in-house market research team, but analyzing those findings and deciphering the real signals behind the data?

That takes a content strategist like me who connects the dots between what research gathers and what viewers on your content platforms finally see.

02 — Turning Competitive Intelligence into a Sales Advantage

A large Industrial IoT manufacturer was losing deals. Their sales head had had enough. The ask was direct: "Understand why we keep losing, and please give me something I can use."

The Challenge

Competition in the field of manufacturing software was fragmented. Everyone was working on IIoT and Cloud, but differed in strategies and reach.

The Solution

I conducted an extensive secondary research on 8-10 competitors, covering their products, GTM strategies, customer base, and financials. I ensured the research covered the full manufacturing software stack: MES, ERP, PLM, and SCADA.

The final delivery was a 100+ slide presentation that gave the sales team a clear, structured view of the competitive landscape.

The Outcome

The sales head's response said it all: "This will go into my permanent sales collateral folder. It's very useful."

The rigour of this project also earned me my Six Sigma Green Belt certification.

The Takeaway

Much of what your competitors post on social platforms and their websites is noise. Do you know why? They’re skilled at keeping competitors at bay even as they attract customers.

End result? You end up with generic content. That’s why you need a strategist already skilled in cutting through the noise, using definitive competitive intel tools. How else will exceptional content, unique to your positioning, will even reach your prospects?

The Writer

Having spoken with sales heads, CXOs, and countless other people in leadership positions for a decade, one thing was clear to me: a lack of alignment existed in high-tech companies, regardless of the company size.

Half the time, salespeople don’t know what product teams are talking about, and the other half of the time, buyers don’t know where to find information (and what to make of it even when found).

If this sounds familiar, then you know what you need: a strategist who is also good with words, aka a seasoned writer.

Fortunately, I learned the aspects of tech writing from some of the best in the industry and have ghostwritten 100+ articles, website pages, and social media posts in six years.

Read through a couple of those business cases where my writing prowess shone through. Hopefully, you’ll walk into something that sounds like, “Ah! But that’s exactly like my challenge.”

01 — A Case Study and Press Release Enlivened from Raw Data

An Australia-based industrial asset management solutions provider was all set to launch their new cloud-based utility mapping platform. They needed an elaborate case study document and a press release article, built from a stack of raw data.

The Challenge

I was given a hurriedly built demo document with several feature details missing. Additionally, I was given a couple of random quotes from the CEO of the company and a brief interview transcript that served as an overview of a case study. Data, as you can imagine, was scattered all over the place.

The Solution

To begin with, I studied the demo document in depth, did my own research on the topic, and looked up a few competitors in the same domain to fill in the missing gaps of information.

Then, I segregated the raw data provided to me into two buckets, one for the case study and the other for the press release.

Finally, I created templates for both forms of content and wrote, keeping in mind their Australian customers.

The Outcome

My client was amazed by the sheer level of detail I captured in the case study and also by the succinctly balanced act of the press release document. He gave me a glowing 5-star rating and recommended me for all future projects.

And the launch? It happened in record time, thanks to the ready-to-go press release.

The Takeaway

You probably have data lying everywhere. Some in your spreadsheets, some in your CRM, some in leadership’s brains and visions, and some in haphazardly created documents.

What you don’t have is someone to make sense of it all and coherently form assets that can be posted on various marketing platforms.

What you need is not just a writer, but a writer who understands your product architecture from a basic block diagram and a brief transcript. A writer who knows which feature goes into which content form, and a writer who reforms the words exactly as your customers need to hear.

A writer, in short, exactly like me.

02 — A Whitepaper for a Disruptive Technology

An AI consulting and development company was venturing into the world of Generative AI, and they wanted a whitepaper based on their new solution in the pipeline.

The Challenge

Generative AI was a fairly new concept at that time. It had only been months since OpenAI’s ChatGPT was launched and threw the entire tech world into disarray. My client, like many other AI firms, was trying to build a Gen AI platform of its own. They had several brilliant ideas, but no one to pen them down methodically.

The Solution

As ChatGPT was fairly new and considerably flawed, it didn’t make sense to understand their tech much. Rather, I went a level deeper. I tried to comprehend the actual workings of a Gen AI framework, from what it was to how it was built.

When it comes to writing a technical paper, there’s no better approach to getting into the core of the innovation. The client supported me with their internal resources on the topic, which I combined with my own research on neural networks and generative adversarial networks (GANs).

My job was fairly simple from thereon: write for a non-technical audience, explaining each term without jargon, and produce something stellar and original, as original as my client’s Gen AI platform.

The Outcome

The whitepaper was well received, and after a few minor edits by their technical head, was published in their internal newsletter. The client praised my deep research and comprehension of a brand-new and disruptive technology.

The Takeaway

If you work in deep tech, you already know the deal. Competitors are launching new products every day, and you panic that if you do not innovate soon enough, you will fall behind.

The other aspect of your panic that probably no one acknowledges is: how do you get the word out that you are, indeed, working on something special, whether as an internal memo or to the outside world?

A writer like me, with a technical background, experience, and the ability to get to the core of tech, is your remedy to such panic.

The Curator

A brilliant writer and a seasoned strategist are your go-to people if you need the world to know just how amazing your B2B tech solution is. But what if you already have a bunch of content, perhaps written by your internal team? What if you think the pieces are brilliant, and yet, they fail to get you any real customers?

This is the phase where you require a curator to step in. And if you already have one person donning the role of a content strategist and a writer, their brilliance in curation, added as a third layer to their expertise, can be that difference between mindless posts and a true pipeline of prospective buyers.

Fortunately, that’s where I come in, with 2+ years of working in a B2B SaaS startup, leading, managing, and curating content that drives growth. Read a couple of such case studies below.

01 — An AI Transformation Platform Blog

The B2B SaaS company had, at its core, an AI Transformation Platform that they wanted to promote across all channels, starting with a first-ever blog.

The Challenge

Several attempts were made in the past to write articles that would serve to serve the top of the funnel for the platform. However, those articles were all shelved for lack of a discerning eye. Everything remained unpublished.

The Solution

As a content curator, leading three freelance writers, I categorized the written material into two industrial buckets, namely Field Service Management (FSM) and Material Management Systems (MMS).

I picked out the crown jewels among the written articles, mapped them with the relevance of our primary ideal customer profile (ICP), and edited them to get them published on the company website.

The Outcome

Curious about the results (as a content curator often is), I built a dashboard by self-learning Google’s very own Looker Studio. The dashboard proved that the blog posts drew a significant number of visitors, and several of them were driven to the Demo page on the company website.

The Takeaway

Too much content confuses buyers. Irrelevant content scares them away.

Your organization may be rich in technology and opinionated contributors, but are they producing content that aligns with what the buyer wants to hear? Are they even reaching those who have zero intention of buying right now, but may consider it someday in the future?

The right content does this, not more content. And how do you know what the right content is? That’s where I step in, the curator who sees it from an outsider’s lens.

02 — A Full Content Stack for Financial Transformation with AI

With changes in positioning, the B2B SaaS AI transformation platform had a new role: transformation of capital for Indian mid-market CXOs. A fresh content stack needed to be built to facilitate the offer’s reach.

The Challenge

Having led the prospects to believe in the utility of the AI transformation platform for core service and manufacturing industries, the challenge was now to talk about the same platform to a completely new ICP, the financial decision-makers.

The Solution

I built the strategy for an entire content stack in the hub-and-spoke format. Key viewpoints were presented through long form blog posts, and parts of which were repurposed for posting on social media.

This included the likes of carousels, images, videos, and text-based posts. The content was written by junior writers, which I then curated to pick out the rising stars that were most likely to gather followers and visitors on various social media channels.

The Outcome

The results were easy to monitor and measure through paid LinkedIn and Meta campaigns. I edited the Looker Studio dashboard built for the earlier projects so it would reflect the attributions from the different social media channels.

The Takeaway

If you already have in-house content assets ready, which are talking successfully to your ICP at regular intervals, you may not need anything new from me.

However, what if your ICP suddenly changes? Or you decide to expand your portfolio? Or you dream of taking your successful product to the next level of buyers? Would your existing content still be able to carry on the conversations? Some of them might, others may need to leave or stay where they were before.

How do you decide? You don’t. This is where I enter the fray with my strategic background and curator experience and tell you which content should go where and who it should be targeted at.
