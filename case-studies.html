Does Your Content Speak Your Buyer's Language?

B2B buyers are tough to please. They are so deep in their own world that they can smell — and reject — content that does not speak to them. What you need is not just a writer, but a writer who is a strong strategist and a curator.

The Strategist

I didn't just wake up one day and become a B2B content strategist for tech domains. First, there was the Master's degree in Computer Engineering. Then, a decade of working with industrial tech leaders — enabling them to improve their sales, strategize their GTM initiatives, and enhance customer satisfaction.

01 — Mapping the Road Ahead for an Automotive Manufacturer

A South Asian automotive manufacturer needed to understand the market landscape before committing to their go-to-market strategy.

The Challenge

  • How safe were the roads?
  • What was the government doing about vehicle and road safety technology?
  • Where were competitors placing their bets on vehicle automation?

The Solution

I conducted a comprehensive primary and secondary research study, interviewing government officials, industry experts, competitors, and internal stakeholders to build a complete picture of the landscape. The analysis was structured using SWOT and AHP frameworks to prioritize findings and surface actionable strategic direction.

The Outcome

The final delivery was a detailed research report and a crisp strategy slide deck that fed directly into the client's go-to-market planning.

The Takeaway

A thorough market study is vital to any successful content marketing project. Analyzing findings and deciphering the real signals behind the data takes a content strategist who connects the dots between what research gathers and what viewers on your content platforms finally see.

02 — Turning Competitive Intelligence into a Sales Advantage

A large Industrial IoT manufacturer was losing deals. Their sales head had had enough. The ask was direct: "Understand why we keep losing, and please give me something I can use."

The Challenge

Competition in the field of manufacturing software was fragmented. Everyone was working on IIoT and Cloud, but differed in strategies and reach.

The Solution

I conducted extensive secondary research on 8–10 competitors, covering their products, GTM strategies, customer base, and financials — across the full manufacturing software stack: MES, ERP, PLM, and SCADA. The final delivery was a 100+ slide presentation giving the sales team a clear, structured view of the competitive landscape.

The Outcome

The sales head's response said it all: "This will go into my permanent sales collateral folder. It's very useful." The rigour of this project also earned me my Six Sigma Green Belt certification.

The Takeaway

Much of what your competitors post online is noise — they're skilled at keeping competitors at bay even as they attract customers. You need a strategist skilled in cutting through that noise with definitive competitive intel tools.

The Writer

Having spoken with sales heads, CXOs, and leadership for a decade, one thing was clear: a lack of alignment existed in high-tech companies regardless of size. Salespeople don't know what product teams are talking about, and buyers don't know where to find information. What you need is a strategist who is also good with words.

01 — A Case Study and Press Release Enlivened from Raw Data

An Australia-based industrial asset management solutions provider was launching their new cloud-based utility mapping platform and needed an elaborate case study and press release — built from scattered raw data.

The Challenge

I was given a hurriedly built demo document with several feature details missing, a couple of random CEO quotes, and a brief interview transcript. Data was scattered all over the place.

The Solution

I studied the demo document in depth, conducted my own research, and looked up competitors to fill information gaps. I then segregated the raw data into two buckets — one for the case study, one for the press release — created templates for both, and wrote with their Australian customers in mind.

The Outcome

My client was amazed by the detail captured in the case study and the succinctly balanced press release. He gave a glowing 5-star rating and recommended me for all future projects. The launch happened in record time.

The Takeaway

You probably have data lying everywhere — in spreadsheets, CRMs, leadership's brains, haphazardly created documents. What you need is a writer who understands your product architecture from a basic block diagram, knows which feature goes into which content form, and reforms the words exactly as your customers need to hear them.

02 — A Whitepaper for a Disruptive Technology

An AI consulting firm was venturing into Generative AI and needed a whitepaper on their new solution — in the weeks following ChatGPT's launch.

The Challenge

Generative AI was a brand-new concept. The client had brilliant ideas but no one to pen them down methodically for a non-technical audience.

The Solution

Rather than working from the tool itself, I went a level deeper: studying the actual workings of a Gen AI framework, from neural networks to generative adversarial networks (GANs). I combined the client's internal resources with my own research, then wrote for a non-technical audience — explaining each concept without jargon.

The Outcome

The whitepaper was well received. After minor edits by the technical head, it was published in the client's internal newsletter. The client praised my deep research and comprehension of a brand-new and disruptive technology.

The Takeaway

If you work in deep tech, competitors are launching new products every day. A writer with a technical background who can get to the core of your innovation — and get the word out clearly — is your remedy to that panic.

The Curator

What if you already have a bunch of content written by your internal team, you believe the pieces are brilliant, and yet they fail to get real customers? This is the phase where a curator steps in — someone who can see from an outsider's lens and determine what the right content is, not just more content.

01 — An AI Transformation Platform Blog

A B2B SaaS company wanted to promote their AI Transformation Platform across all channels, starting with a first-ever blog. Several previous attempts had produced articles that were shelved — everything remained unpublished.

The Solution

As content curator leading three freelance writers, I categorized written material into two industrial buckets: Field Service Management (FSM) and Material Management Systems (MMS). I identified the strongest articles, mapped them to the primary ICP, and edited them to publication standard.

The Outcome

I built a Looker Studio dashboard by self-learning the tool. The dashboard confirmed that the blog posts drew significant visitor numbers — and several were driven directly to the Demo page on the company website.

The Takeaway

Too much content confuses buyers. Irrelevant content scares them away. The right content does neither — and knowing which content is "right" is precisely where a skilled curator adds value.

02 — A Full Content Stack for Financial Transformation with AI

The same AI transformation platform repositioned toward a new ICP: financial decision-makers in India's mid-market. An entirely fresh content stack was needed.

The Solution

I built the strategy for an entire hub-and-spoke content stack. Long-form blog posts covered key viewpoints, with parts repurposed across social media as carousels, images, videos, and text posts. I personally authored 4 anchor posts targeting CFOs and COOs, then curated junior writers' output to identify the pieces most likely to gather followers and drive traffic.

The Outcome

Results were tracked through paid LinkedIn and Meta campaigns via an updated Looker Studio dashboard reflecting attributions across all social channels.

The Takeaway

If your ICP changes, or you expand your portfolio, or you take your product to a new buyer tier — your existing content may not carry the conversation forward. Knowing what stays, what goes, and what needs to be rebuilt is where strategic curation earns its value.
